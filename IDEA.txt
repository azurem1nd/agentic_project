IDEA 1 - MUSIC
CONTEXT:
Streaming platforms organize music the way a record store would: by artist, 
genre, era, mood-tag playlists built for generic contexts ("Chill," "Workout," 
"Focus"). But that's not how people actually relate to their own listening history.
A song from a completely different genre and decade can sit next to another 
because they both mean "the summer i turned 18" or "driving home 
from college at 2am." That organizing logic is entirely personal, built
from memory and repetition, not metadata.

Meanwhile, every platform already has the raw signal to reconstruct this 
— timestamps, replay patterns, skip behavior, sudden drop-offs or 
re-emergences of a track after months — but none of it is surfaced back to the 
user in a way that means anything. It's used internally to feed recommendation 
engines, never to help someone understand their own listening life. 
The result is that years of listening data accumulate invisibly, 
and a user's only way to revisit "what music got me through that period" 
is manual memory or a playlist they happened to name well at the time.
